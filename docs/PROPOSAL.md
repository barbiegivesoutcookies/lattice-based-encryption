## 1. Abstract

The security of RSA, Diffie–Hellman, and elliptic-curve cryptography rests on the conjectured intractability of integer factorization and the discrete logarithm problem for *classical* computers. This assumption collapses against a sufficiently large fault-tolerant quantum computer: Shor's algorithm [Shor 1994; Shor 1997] solves both problems in polynomial time by reducing them to period finding under the quantum Fourier transform. Every public-key infrastructure built on these assumptions is therefore retroactively broken by any future cryptographically relevant quantum computer ("harvest now, decrypt later" adversaries compound the urgency today).

Lattice-based cryptography offers the leading migration path. The foundational hard problem is the **Shortest Vector Problem (SVP)**: given a lattice $\mathcal{L} = \{ \sum_i z_i \mathbf{b}_i : z_i \in \mathbb{Z} \} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^m$ generated by a basis $\{\mathbf{b}_1, \dots, \mathbf{b}_n\}$, find a nonzero lattice vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathcal{L} \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\}$ minimizing the Euclidean norm $\|\mathbf{v}\|_2$. Its approximate variant $\gamma$-SVP asks for a nonzero vector within approximation factor $\gamma$ of $\lambda_1(\mathcal{L})$, the length of the true shortest vector. No polynomial-time algorithm is known for either problem on classical *or* quantum computers; the best known quantum speedups are Grover-type quadratic accelerations applied inside classical sieving algorithms, which improve asymptotic exponents but do not yield polynomial-time solutions. It must be stated with full epistemic precision: **there is no proof that SVP (or any lattice problem used in cryptography) is hard for quantum computers**. The quantum resistance of NTRU is a *conjecture*, supported by three decades of failed cryptanalytic attempts and by the structural fact that lattice problems appear immune to the periodic-structure exploitation that powers Shor's algorithm — but it remains a conjecture, not an established mathematical fact.

This project implements, from first principles and without external cryptographic libraries, the **NTRU** cryptosystem (Hoffstein, Pipher, Silverman, 1996/1998) over the polynomial quotient ring $R = \mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^N - 1)$. The design converts a *ring-theoretic trapdoor* into a lattice trapdoor: the public key conceals a very short vector in a high-dimensional convolution modular lattice, and decryption succeeds exactly when no coefficient wraps around the modulus $q$. Because this wrap-around condition is probabilistic rather than guaranteed, NTRU decryption failure analysis is treated here as a first-class mathematical object: we derive the variance of the decryption noise term, state both a rigorous Cantelli bound and the standard heuristic Chernoff/Gaussian bound used throughout the NTRU literature, and validate both empirically at deliberately stressed parameter boundaries.

The engineering contribution comprises: (i) an object-oriented `NTRUEngine` encapsulating cyclic convolution, Extended Euclidean inversion over the finite field $\mathbb{F}_p[X]$, and Hensel-lifting inversion modulo $q = 2^k$; (ii) a fully specified ternary plaintext codec; (iii) a FastAPI microservice exposing key generation, encryption, and decryption; (iv) a seeded `unittest` suite including a 200-iteration randomized round-trip correctness proof-by-execution and an empirical decryption-failure-rate measurement compared against the theoretical bound; and (v) a `timeit`/`matplotlib` benchmarking suite comparing the lattice scheme against a from-scratch textbook RSA baseline across three parameter tiers.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

### 2.1 The quotient ring $R = \mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^N - 1)$

Fix a prime integer $N$ and let

$$
R \;=\; \mathbb{Z}[X] \big/ (X^N - 1).
$$

Elements of $R$ are residue classes of integer polynomials modulo the principal ideal $(X^N - 1)$; each class has a unique representative

$$
a(X) = a_0 + a_1 X + a_2 X^2 + \cdots + a_{N-1} X^{N-1}, \qquad a_i \in \mathbb{Z}.
$$

We identify $a$ with its coefficient vector $(a_0, \dots, a_{N-1}) \in \mathbb{Z}^N$. Addition is coefficient-wise. Multiplication is polynomial multiplication followed by reduction via the defining relation $X^N \equiv 1$, i.e. **cyclic convolution**:

$$
c(X) = a(X)\, b(X) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c_k \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} a_i \, b_{(k-i) \bmod N}, \qquad k = 0, \dots, N-1.
$$

For coprime moduli $p$ and $q$ (with $p$ small and $q$ large), define the quotient rings

$$
R_p = R / pR = (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})[X]/(X^N - 1), \qquad R_q = R / qR = (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})[X]/(X^N - 1).
$$

Because $p$ is prime, $\mathbb{F}_p[X]$ is a Euclidean domain and the Extended Euclidean Algorithm applies in $R_p$: a polynomial $f$ is invertible in $R_p$ if and only if $\gcd(f,\, X^N - 1) = 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[X]$. Since $N$ is prime, $X^N - 1 = (X-1)\,\Phi_N(X)$ over any field, where $\Phi_N$ is the $N$-th cyclotomic polynomial; hence $f$ fails to be invertible precisely when $(X-1) \mid f$ or when $f$ shares a factor with $\Phi_N \bmod p$.

Because $q = 2^k$ is composite, $\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$ is **not** a field: it contains zero divisors ($2^{k-1} \cdot 2 \equiv 0$), so polynomial division is not generally available and the Euclidean Algorithm does not directly apply in $R_q$. Inversion modulo $q$ is instead achieved by **Hensel lifting** from the inverse modulo $p$ (Section 3.3).

### 2.2 Ternary polynomials and key generation

Define the ternary set $\mathcal{T}(d_1, d_2) \subset R$:

$$
\mathcal{T}(d_1, d_2) \;=\; \left\{\, f \in R \;:\; f \text{ has exactly } d_1 \text{ coefficients equal to } +1,\ d_2 \text{ equal to } -1,\ \text{the rest } 0 \,\right\}.
$$

**Key Generation.**

1. Sample $f \in \mathcal{T}(d_f,\, d_f - 1)$. The asymmetric choice forces $f(1) = d_f - (d_f - 1) = 1$, guaranteeing that $(X - 1)$ does not divide $f$ over every field, eliminating one systematic obstruction to invertibility.
2. Compute $F_p = f^{-1} \in R_p$ (Extended Euclidean Algorithm over $\mathbb{F}_p[X]$). If $f$ is not invertible, resample.
3. Compute $F_q = f^{-1} \in R_q$ (Hensel lifting).
4. Sample $g \in \mathcal{T}(d_g, d_g)$.
5. Publish the **public key**

$$
h \;=\; p \cdot F_q \cdot g \pmod{q} \;\in\; R_q,
$$

   and retain the **private key** triple $(f,\ F_p,\ F_q)$.

### 2.3 Ideal mapping and the structure of the trapdoor

The map $\psi_h : R \to R_q$, $u \mapsto u \cdot h \bmod q$, is multiplication by $h$ in the quotient ring; equivalently, it is projection onto the ideal $(h) \subset R_q$. Substituting the definition of $h$:

$$
f \cdot h \;=\; f \cdot p \cdot f^{-1} \cdot g \;=\; p \cdot g \pmod{q}.
$$

This single identity is the entire trapdoor. An encryptor who multiplies by $h$ is unknowingly operating in coordinates where multiplication by the secret $f$ annihilates the randomness down to the small element $p\,g$:

$$
e = r \cdot h + m \pmod q \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f \cdot e \;=\; p\,(r \cdot g) + f \cdot m \pmod q .
$$

Geometrically, $h$ determines the **convolutional modular lattice**

$$
L_h \;=\; \bigl\{\, (u, v) \in \mathbb{Z}^{2N} \;:\; v \equiv u \cdot h \pmod q \,\bigr\},
$$

a rank-$2N$ lattice containing the identity-like sublattice $q\mathbb{Z}^N \times q\mathbb{Z}^N$, hence of determinant $q^N$. By construction, $(f, p\,g) \in L_h$ is an extraordinarily short vector: $\|(f, pg)\|$ is on the order of $\sqrt{(2d_f - 1) + p^2 (2d_g)}$, while a random point of $L_h$ has expected norm on the order of $\sqrt{N q / 3}$ by the Gaussian heuristic. Recovering $(f,g)$ from $h$ is therefore equivalent to solving a bounded-distance decoding / short-vector instance on $L_h$. The conjectured hardness of this task against both classical and quantum adversaries is the security foundation of NTRU; as stated above, this hardness is **conjectured**, not proven.

### 2.4 Encryption and decryption

**Encryption.** For message $m \in R$ with small coefficients (Section 4 encodes plaintext into coefficients of $\{-1, 0, +1\}$) and fresh blinding polynomial $r \in \mathcal{T}(d_r, d_r)$:

$$
\boxed{\; e \;=\; r \cdot h + m \pmod{q}. \;}
$$

Fresh randomness $r$ per encryption is mandatory: two ciphertexts sharing $r$ satisfy $e_1 - e_2 = m_1 - m_2 \pmod q$, leaking the message difference directly.

**Decryption.**

1. Compute $a = f \cdot e \bmod q$. Over the integers,

$$
a \;\equiv\; \underbrace{p \cdot r \cdot g}_{\text{noise}} \;+\; \underbrace{f \cdot m}_{\text{signal}} \pmod{q}.
$$

2. **Centered lift** each coefficient of $a$ into the symmetric window $(-q/2,\, q/2]$: replace $a_k \in [0, q)$ by $a_k - q$ if $a_k > q/2$.
3. Reduce modulo $p$: the noise term $p\,r\,g$ vanishes identically, leaving $f \cdot m \bmod p$.
4. Multiply by $F_p$: $\; m' = F_p \cdot (f m) = m \pmod p$.

## 3. Decryption Failure Analysis

### 3.1 Why decryption is probabilistic

Step 2 of decryption is the sole locus of failure. The integer polynomial

$$
C \;=\; p \cdot r \cdot g + f \cdot m \;\in\; \mathbb{Z}[X]/(X^N - 1)
$$

is determined only modulo $q$ by the computation $a = f e \bmod q$. Centered lifting recovers the *true* integer coefficients of $C$ if and only if every coefficient satisfies

$$
-\frac{q}{2} < C_k \le \frac{q}{2} \qquad \text{for all } k \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}.
$$

If even one coefficient exceeds the window, it wraps around $q$, and the subsequent reduction modulo $p$ mixes wrapped and unwrapped contributions: the recovered $m'$ differs from $m$ in an unpredictable subset of coefficients. Decryption is therefore **not guaranteed** — it succeeds with probability governed by the tail of the distribution of $\|C\|_\infty$, which in turn depends on the parameter triple $(N, p, q)$ and the Hamming weights $(d_f, d_g, d_r)$ through the norm bounds of $f, g, r, m$.

### 3.2 Variance of the noise-plus-signal term

Model $f \in \mathcal{T}(d_f, d_f-1)$, $g \in \mathcal{T}(d_g, d_g)$, $r \in \mathcal{T}(d_r, d_r)$ as independent uniform samples, and model the message $m$ as having i.i.d. coefficients uniform on $\{-1, 0, +1\}$ (this matches the codec of Section 4, giving $\operatorname{Var}(m_i) = 2/3$). Each coordinate of a ternary vector drawn this way is a centered random variable with variance

$$
\sigma_f^2 = \frac{2 d_f - 1}{N}, \qquad \sigma_g^2 = \frac{2 d_g}{N}, \qquad \sigma_r^2 = \frac{2 d_r}{N}, \qquad \sigma_m^2 = \frac{2}{3}.
$$

A convolution coefficient $(u \cdot v)_k = \sum_i u_i v_{(k-i) \bmod N}$ is a sum of $N$ independent products of independent centered variables, hence

$$
\operatorname{Var}\bigl[(u \cdot v)_k\bigr] \;=\; N \, \sigma_u^2 \, \sigma_v^2 .
$$

Applying this to both terms of $C$ and summing (independence between $r\,g$ and $f\,m$):

$$
\boxed{\;
\sigma_C^2 \;=\; N \left( \sigma_f^2 \sigma_m^2 \;+\; p^2 \, \sigma_r^2 \sigma_g^2 \right)
\;=\; N \left( \frac{(2d_f - 1)}{N}\cdot\frac{2}{3} \;+\; p^2 \cdot \frac{2 d_r}{N} \cdot \frac{2 d_g}{N} \right).
\;}
$$

### 3.3 Tail bounds

**Rigorous bound (Cantelli / one-sided Chebyshev).** Without any distributional assumption beyond mean zero and variance $\sigma_C^2$,

$$
\Pr\bigl[|C_k| \ge q/2\bigr] \;\le\; \frac{4\,\sigma_C^2}{q^2},
\qquad
\Pr[\text{failure}] \;\le\; \min\!\left(1,\; \frac{4 N \sigma_C^2}{q^2}\right).
$$

This bound is unconditionally valid but far too loose to guide parameter selection: for the default parameter set below it evaluates to approximately $0.64$ after the union bound, despite empirical failure rates being zero over hundreds of thousands of trials.

**Heuristic Chernoff/Gaussian bound (standard in the NTRU literature, cf. IEEE P1363.1 estimation practice).** Convolution coefficients are sums of many small independent terms; modeling them as Gaussian with variance $\sigma_C^2$ yields the sub-Gaussian tail

$$
\Pr\bigl[|C_k| \ge q/2\bigr] \;\le\; 2 \exp\!\left( -\frac{q^2}{8\,\sigma_C^2} \right),
$$

and the union bound over all $N$ coefficients gives the standard **decryption failure probability estimate**

$$
\boxed{\;
\Pr[\text{failure}] \;\lesssim\; 2 N \exp\!\left( -\frac{q^2}{8\,\sigma_C^2} \right).
\;}
$$

*Epistemic status:* this is a heuristic estimate validated empirically, not a proven theorem; the independence/Gaussian assumption on convolution coefficients is well-supported by experiment but unproven. Both bounds are implemented in `NTRUEngine.decryption_failure_bound` and `NTRUEngine.decryption_variance`, and the empirical suite (`tests/test_ntru.py`) measures observed failure rates against the heuristic bound at deliberately stressed parameters.

### 3.4 Parameter selection consequences

For the default academic parameter set $(N, p, q, d_f, d_g, d_r) = (509, 3, 2048, 216, 72, 72)$:

$$
\sigma_f^2 = \tfrac{431}{509} \approx 0.847, \quad
\sigma_g^2 = \sigma_r^2 = \tfrac{144}{509} \approx 0.283, \quad
\sigma_C^2 \approx 509\,(0.847 \cdot \tfrac{2}{3} + 9 \cdot 0.283^2) \approx 654,
$$

$$
\Pr[\text{failure}] \;\lesssim\; 1018 \cdot \exp\!\left(-\tfrac{2048^2}{8 \cdot 654}\right) \;=\; 1018 \cdot e^{-801} \;<\; 10^{-300}.
$$

Parameter selection controls failure probability through three levers: increasing $q$ enters the exponent quadratically; decreasing the densities $d_f, d_g, d_r$ shrinks $\sigma_C^2$ linearly-to-quadratically; and constraining message coefficients to $\{-1,0,+1\}$ (rather than $[0, p)$) minimizes $\sigma_m^2$. The art of NTRU parameterization is balancing this failure budget against the lattice dimension $2N$ and modulus $q$ that determine security level — smaller $q$ and sparser keys ease efficiency but tighten the failure margin.

## 4. Methodology: From Abstract Algebra to Discrete Computation

### 4.1 Data representation

Ring elements are stored as NumPy `int64` arrays of length exactly $N$, index $i$ holding the coefficient of $X^i$ (little-endian degree order). All reductions are coefficient-wise with representatives in $[0, m)$; centered lifting maps to $[-m/2, m/2)$. Overflow safety: intermediate convolution sums are bounded by $N \cdot (q-1)^2$, far inside int64 range for all parameters considered.

### 4.2 Multiplication: O(N²) cyclic convolution, and why NTT is unavailable

Multiplication is implemented as schoolbook cyclic convolution, $O(N^2)$ ring operations, vectorized over NumPy rows:

```python
for i in range(N):
    acc += a[i] * np.roll(b, i)
```

The Number Theoretic Transform would reduce this to $O(N \log N)$, but it requires a primitive $N$-th root of unity in $\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$. For $q = 2^k$ none exists: the unit group satisfies $(\mathbb{Z}/2^k\mathbb{Z})^\times \cong C_2 \times C_{2^{k-2}}$, of order $2^{k-1}$ — a 2-group containing no element of odd prime order $N$. Consequently no NTT-based acceleration is applicable in this modulus regime, and the quadratic algorithm is the correct engineering choice. (Alternative regimes — prime $q \equiv 1 \pmod{2N}$ enabling NTT, or Schönhage–Strassen-style ring extensions — are noted as future optimization work.)

### 4.3 Inversion modulo p: Extended Euclidean Algorithm over a genuine finite field

Since $p = 3$ is prime, $\mathbb{F}_3[X]$ is a Euclidean domain. The engine computes $g, u, v$ with $u f + v (X^N - 1) = g$ via the polynomial Extended Euclidean Algorithm (vectorized long division; every nonzero leading coefficient is invertible because the coefficient ring is a field). If $\deg g = 0$, then $f^{-1} = g(0)^{-1} u \bmod (X^N - 1)$; otherwise $f$ is a zero divisor and key generation resamples $f$.

### 4.4 Inversion modulo q = 2^k: Hensel lifting

The Extended Euclidean Algorithm is **not** applicable modulo composite $q$: $\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$ is not a field, division by non-invertible leading coefficients is undefined, and Bezout identities need not exist. Inversion is instead achieved by **Hensel lifting with binary correction**, doubling the validity modulus from $t = p$ up to $t = pq$.

Write $\mathbf{1} = (1, 0, \dots, 0)$ for the multiplicative identity of $R$. Suppose the invariant $f\, F^{(t)} \equiv \mathbf{1} \pmod t$ holds. Reducing modulo $2t$,

$$
f\, F^{(t)} \;=\; \mathbf{1} + t\, e \pmod{2t}, \qquad e \in \{0,1\}^N,
$$

where the division by $t$ is exact coefficientwise. Seeking a correction $F^{(2t)} = F^{(t)} + t\, u$ with $u \in \{0,1\}^N$:

$$
f\, F^{(2t)} \;=\; f\, F^{(t)} + t\, u f \;\equiv\; \mathbf{1} + t\,(e + u f) \pmod{2t},
$$

so the invariant is restored modulo $2t$ precisely when $u \equiv e\, f^{-1} \pmod 2$, where $f^{-1} \in R_2$ is computed once per key generation via the Extended Euclidean Algorithm over $\mathbb{F}_2[X]$ (a genuine field). A remark on correctness: the naive squaring form $F^{(2t)} = F^{(t)}(2 - f F^{(t)}) \bmod 2t$ is *invalid* here — squaring lifts validity from mod $t$ to mod $t^2$, and congruence mod $t^2$ implies congruence mod $2t$ only when $t$ is even; starting from odd $t = p$, intermediate reduction modulo $2t$ destroys the invariant. The explicit correction step above is the mathematically sound doubling lift.

Starting at $t = p$ and applying the step $\log_2 q$ times reaches $t = p q$ exactly (since $q = 2^k$); because $q \mid pq$, reduction modulo $q$ then gives the exact inverse in $R_q$, verified by the engine via an explicit $f \cdot F_q \equiv \mathbf{1} \pmod q$ check before any key is accepted.

### 4.5 Plaintext encoding (normative specification)

Plaintext bytes are mapped onto ternary message polynomials as follows:

1. Prefix the byte string with its 4-byte big-endian length.
2. Split into blocks of $B = \lfloor N/6 \rfloor$ bytes (84 bytes for $N = 509$); zero-pad the final block.
3. Expand each byte $b$ into six base-3 digits, least significant first: $b = \sum_{d=0}^{5} \delta_d 3^d$ (six digits suffice since $3^5 = 243 < 256 \le 729 = 3^6$).
4. Map digits through the balanced ternary embedding $0 \mapsto 0$, $1 \mapsto +1$, $2 \mapsto -1$, filling consecutive coefficient slots; residual high-degree slots ($N \bmod 6$ of them) are fixed to zero.

Decoding concatenates recovered blocks, reassembles bytes, validates the length prefix, and truncates padding. The balanced embedding minimizes $\sigma_m^2$ and hence the decryption failure rate of Section 3.

### 4.6 Reference parameter set

| Symbol | Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| $N$ | 509 | Ring dimension; prime |
| $p$ | 3 | Small plaintext modulus |
| $q$ | 2048 = $2^{11}$ | Large ciphertext modulus |
| $d_f$ | 216 | $+1$ count in $f$ (with $215$ entries of $-1$) |
| $d_g$ | 72 | $+1/-1$ counts in $g$ |
| $d_r$ | 72 | $+1/-1$ counts in blinding polynomial $r$ |
| $B$ | 84 | Plaintext bytes per message polynomial |

These follow the structural conventions of the EESS/NTRU parameter families and are intended for study and benchmarking; they are not asserted to meet any certified concrete-security profile, and raw NTRU encryption as specified here is IND-CPA secure only under the conjectured hardness of the underlying lattice problems. IND-CCA security requires an additional transform (NAEP or Fujisaki–Okamoto), which is out of scope for this project and listed as future work.

### 4.7 Complexity summary

| Operation | Cost |
|---|---|
| Multiplication in $R$ | $O(N^2)$ |
| Encryption / decryption | $O(N^2)$ (two convolutions plus linear work) |
| Inversion mod $p$ (EEA) | $O(N^2)$ expected |
| Inversion mod $q$ (Hensel) | $O(N^2 \log q)$ |
| Key generation | dominated by inversions |
| Memory | $O(N)$ |

## 5. Systems Component and Benchmarking Architecture

### 5.1 Architectural data pipeline

Data flows top-down through five strictly separated layers:

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 1: USER INTERFACE                                                  |
|   HTTP client / curl / OpenAPI UI (Swagger) issuing JSON requests        |
+---------------------------------|----------------------------------------+
                                  v
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 2: API GATEWAY (FastAPI + Pydantic)                                |
|   Route dispatch, schema validation, type coercion, HTTP error mapping   |
+---------------------------------|----------------------------------------+
                                  v
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 3: SERVICE ORCHESTRATION                                           |
|   TernaryCodec: UTF-8 -> length prefix -> base-3 -> balanced ternary     |
|   Block scheduling, key serialization/deserialization                    |
+---------------------------------|----------------------------------------+
                                  v
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 4: CRYPTOGRAPHIC CORE (NTRUEngine)                                 |
|   Keygen / Encrypt / Decrypt pipelines; modular inverses (EEA, Hensel)   |
+---------------------------------|----------------------------------------+
                                  v
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER 5: POLYNOMIAL RING ARRAY PROCESSING UNIT (NumPy int64 arrays)      |
|   Cyclic convolution O(N^2), coefficient-wise modular reduction,         |
|   centered lifting                                                       |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

Each layer communicates only through immutable value objects (JSON documents, dataclass key objects, `int64` arrays), giving deterministic unit-testability at every boundary.

### 5.2 Benchmarking methodology

The suite (`benchmarks/benchmark.py`) uses `timeit.Timer.repeat` with minimum-of-repeats aggregation, a fixed seed (`SEED = 20260822`), and a fixed 64-byte workload. Three NTRU tiers — $(107, 512)$, $(167, 512)$, $(509, 2048)$ — are measured against textbook RSA at 512, 1024, and 2048 bits. Results are emitted as a console table, `benchmark_results.csv`, and a log-scale grouped bar chart `benchmark_results.png`. Expected qualitative outcome: NTRU key generation and encryption scale near-linearly-quadratically in $N$ and remain orders of magnitude faster than RSA key generation at comparable classical security estimates, while RSA encryption benefits from the tiny public exponent $e = 65537$; RSA decryption cost grows steeply with modulus size, whereas NTRU decryption grows only with $N$. These comparisons are performance observations, not security claims: RSA's hardness is broken by Shor's algorithm on a large quantum computer, while NTRU's quantum resistance is conjectured.
